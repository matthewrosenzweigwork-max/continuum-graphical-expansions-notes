# Syllabus

## Aim and format
**Graphical Expansions in Continuum Statistical Mechanics: Combinatorics, Analysis, and Alternative Representations**

Six graduate 90-minute lectures and two advanced workshops. Core notes target roughly 75–100 pages, with complete proofs taking precedence. The audience is graduate students and researchers in PDE/probability without extensive graph or statistical-mechanics background.

The aim is both practical mastery of expansions and understanding why different combinatorial organizations represent the same object. Throughout, distinguish grand-canonical, canonical, and mean-field regimes, and formal identities, convergent expansions, and asymptotic expansions.

## Core lectures
1. **Graphs, partitions, and connectedness.** Graph vocabulary, set partitions, connected components, exponential formula, and moment–cumulant inversion. Prove the combinatorial identities and work out small examples with all factorials.
2. **Continuum Mayer expansions.** Continuum partition functions, Mayer bonds, and the connected-graph formula for the grand-canonical logarithm. Explain first the algebraic identity and then the analytic justification.
3. **Correlations and cumulants.** Factorial moment densities, truncated/Ursell correlations, generating functionals, and connected graph expansions. Reconcile notation and normalization across representations.
4. **Trees and convergence.** Start with repulsive continuum interactions having an integrable Mayer bond. Prove a complete, explicit, uniform-in-volume cluster-expansion theorem, including convergence and interchange arguments. Explain separately the additional complications of stable attractive interactions.
5. **Alternative representations.** Partitions, trees, Kirkwood–Salsburg equations, and operator/inductive organization. Prove precise equivalences where claimed, and distinguish exact reorganizations from upper bounds.
6. **Canonical asymptotics and applications.** Fixed-particle expansions, power counting, mean-field organization, and a nontrivial controlled remainder. Separate small activity, low density, and fixed-particle weak coupling.

## Advanced workshops
### A. Forest interpolation and recursive organization
Forest interpolation, graph-to-tree transformations, and recursive expansions. Compare the Erdős–Schlein–Yau and Klainerman–Machedon board-game organizations carefully; do not claim a direct Mayer correspondence without a precise construction.

### B. Singular interactions and asymptotics
Coulomb/Riesz interactions, regularization, U-statistics, Wick organization, and modulated-partition asymptotics. Explain failures or nonuniformity of classical tools. Separate formal corrections from rigorous asymptotics and controlled remainders.

## Mathematical standards
- Supply complete proofs of principal statements, with hypotheses and parameter dependence explicit.
- Justify sum/integral/limit exchanges and normalization conventions.
- Include concrete small-graph computations.
- Establish at least one complete continuum convergence theorem.
- State which alternative descriptions are equivalent and which only bound the same quantity.
- Keep research-facing motivations distinct from established theorems.
- Include exercises and solutions, diagram sources, and a notation glossary.

## Literature direction
The initial reading direction includes Jansen's *Cluster Expansions for Gibbs Point Processes* and the canonical approach of Pulvirenti–Tsagkarogiannis. Exact editions, bibliographic metadata, theorem locations, and applicability are to be verified in the literature dossier before citations or theorem claims are frozen.
